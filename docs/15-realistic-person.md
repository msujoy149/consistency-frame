# Realistic Person Video — From a Photo to a Real Moving Person

A real person's photo becomes a realistic video of that person moving and speaking naturally, like a normal real person. The user supplies a clear, well-lit photo, face to camera — and the video shows that person walking, gesturing, turning their head, blinking, and speaking, in their own language, with lip-sync matched to that language's sounds. The photo is used exactly as given: the face, structure, hands, and feet never change. Only movement and speech are added.

This is not 2D or 3D animation, and not live-action footage of a real shoot. Animation styles (2D anime, 3D stylized CG) live in the cinematic-power skill's style lock; real filmed performance lives in the live-action skill. The realistic person video is its own type: a still photo of a real person, made to move and speak like that person. A talking person with a still mouth is a defect, never a delivery. Realism target 100%, with 99.9% as the floor.

## Intake

The user supplies a real person's photo — clear, well-lit, face to camera. One clear photo is enough; several angles are more reliable. A real person's photo and voice need consent: animate a real person only with that person's permission, for the agreed use, with a consent record.

## Identity, movement, speech, realism

The person in the photo stays that person — never beautified, reshaped, or redesigned. Movement is natural: walks, gestures, head turns, blinks, posture shifts, weight shifting from foot to foot — motivated by what the person is doing and saying, never posed, never stiff, never cartoonish. Speech follows the person's own language and its native pattern, with lip-sync matched to that language's sounds. Skin, hair, and cloth behave like the real thing; light is natural; no plastic skin, no cartoon look.

## QC

Before delivery: face vs the supplied photo, movement naturalness, lip-sync per line, and no defects (extra fingers, merged faces, bent limbs, broken objects). A video that fails any check is regenerated, not delivered — and if realistic video from a photo is not supported on the platform, say so plainly instead of promising it.
