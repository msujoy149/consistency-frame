# 2D Explainer Animation — One Idea, Told in Simple Pictures

A short video that teaches one idea through simple scenes and visual metaphors. Flat 2D cartoon / stick-figure explainer, vertical short-form: a fuller cartoon figure for the real person, simple stick figures for the abstract ideas. Every abstract idea becomes a character or a place — internal conflict is two figures arguing inside the brain, an obstacle is a mountain with a face, the brain itself is a friendly thinking companion. One idea per scene, ~10 s per scene, 4–6 scenes for a short video. The story is told by visuals and expression, with almost no on-screen text.

This is not anime, not 3D CG, not live-action, not a music video, not a trailer. Anime and 3D CG styles live in the cinematic-power skill's style lock; this is its own type: flat 2D vector, thick black outlines, flat colour fills, warm neutral backgrounds, a small palette. The camera moves slowly — continuous push-ins inside scenes, slow pans between ideas — and scenes flow by camera movement, not by hard cuts. Faces change (stressed, tired, smiling, worried, angry, confident) while the bodies stay simple: expression carries the meaning.

## Style lock

Flat 2D vector cartoon: thick black outlines, flat fills, no gradients on characters, warm neutral backgrounds, a small palette. Two character designs, kept apart — the fuller cartoon figure is the real person, the stick figures are the abstract ideas. Never mix the two designs, and never let anime styling, a 3D look, or photorealism leak in.

## Metaphor system

Every abstract idea becomes a character or a place. A viewer who mutes the video still gets the idea — if a scene needs its sound to be understood, the metaphor is wrong.

## Structure and camera

One idea per scene, ~10 s per scene, 4–6 scenes for a short video. Readable objects that each say one thing (deadline calendar, clock, laptop, books). Slow continuous push-ins inside scenes, slow pans between ideas; only a few scene changes in a short video.

## QC

Before delivery: the style lock holds across every scene (same outlines, same palette, same two character designs); the metaphor stays readable with the sound off; no anime styling, no 3D look, no photorealism. A video that fails any check is regenerated, not delivered — and if the platform cannot hold the flat 2D style across scenes, say so plainly instead of promising it.
