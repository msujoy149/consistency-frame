# 2D Explainer Animation — One Idea, Told in Simple Pictures

A short video that teaches one idea through simple scenes and visual metaphors. Flat 2D cartoon / stick-figure explainer, vertical short-form. It is NOT anime, NOT 3D CG, NOT live-action, NOT a music video, NOT a trailer.

## 1. Style Lock — Flat 2D Vector, Two Character Designs

- Flat 2D vector cartoon: thick black outlines, flat colour fills, no gradients on characters, warm neutral backgrounds, a small palette (a few strong colours).
- Two character designs, kept apart: a fuller cartoon figure for the real person; simple stick figures (round head, dot eyes, stick limbs) for abstract ideas. The simple figures ARE the abstract ideas; the fuller figure is the real person. Never mix the two designs.
- No anime styling, no 3D look, no photorealism. If the user asks for an explainer or teaching animation, this is the explainer style — the anime and 3D CG styles live in the `cinematic-power` skill's style lock instead, and kids & family animation lives in the `kids-family-animation` skill.

## 2. Metaphor System — Every Abstract Idea Becomes a Character or a Place

- Internal conflict = two figures arguing inside the brain.
- An obstacle = a mountain with a face.
- The brain itself = a friendly thinking companion.
- Every metaphor must be readable without sound: a viewer who mutes the video still gets the idea. If a scene needs its sound to be understood, the metaphor is wrong — fix it before delivery.

## 3. Structure — One Idea Per Scene

- One idea per scene, ~10 s per scene, 4–6 scenes for a short video.
- Readable objects that each say one thing (deadline calendar, clock, laptop, books) — one object, one meaning, never a cluttered frame.
- The story is told by visuals and expression, not by narration alone.

## 4. Camera — Slow Movement, Few Cuts

- Slow continuous push-ins inside scenes; slow pans between ideas.
- Scenes flow by camera movement, not by hard cuts — only a few scene changes in a short video.

## 5. Expression Carries the Meaning

- Faces change (stressed, tired, smiling, worried, angry, confident) while the bodies stay simple.
- Expression changes with the idea, not at random: the face shows what the idea means at that moment.

## 6. Text — Almost None

- Almost no on-screen text. The story is told by visuals and expression.
- On-screen labels only where one word says the whole idea (for example DEADLINE on a calendar).

## 7. QC — Style Lock Holds, Metaphor Stays Readable

Before delivering any explainer video, run all three checks:

- **Style lock holds across every scene** — same outlines, same palette, same two character designs.
- **Metaphor stays readable with the sound off** — a muted viewer still gets the idea.
- **No style leaking in** — no anime styling, no 3D look, no photorealism.

Do not deliver a video that fails any check without telling the user what failed.

## 8. Honest Limits — Say Plainly What Cannot Be Done

If the platform cannot hold the flat 2D style across scenes, say so plainly instead of promising it — and say what it can do instead. Never claim a video is a flat 2D explainer when it is a slideshow with a voiceover, or when the style drifts between scenes.
