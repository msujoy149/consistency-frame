# Live-Action & VFX

Animation can be directed frame by frame. Live action cannot: a real body tires, a real animal does what it does, and a CG creature must live in light that was never on set. This skill adds the six systems that make live-action and VFX hybrid footage hold together — a real human performance, CG creatures inside live footage, water and wetness continuity, practical locations extended with CG, stunt choreography, and real-animal performance.

Nothing here replaces the existing systems. The visual style bible (`production-systems` skill, A1), script-driven camera angles (A12), editing rhythm (`humanized-production` skill, 30.10), script-driven colour grading (28.7), the character registry (`character-consistency` skill), the cinematic-power layer (style lock, creature performance, trailer structure, scale and colour scripting), and the episode QC checklist all still apply, unchanged. This skill adds only the live-action and VFX layer on top of that foundation.

## 1. Live-Action Human Performance — A Real Body Performs

A real body performs: micro-expressions, breath, fatigue, weight. The performance must be physically possible for a real human — no impossible poses, no weightless landings, no effortless lifts of heavy things.

Rules:

- **Effort shows.** Rowing tires arms, climbing tires legs, running leaves the actor breathless. If the action is hard, the body must show it: breathing changes, posture sags, hands shake slightly. An actor who has just run and breathes normally is a defect.
- **Micro-expressions carry the scene.** Determination, fear, wonder, laughter live in the small movements — a tightening jaw, a glance held a beat too long, a breath before speaking. The close-up is where the performance is checked, not the wide shot.
- **Sweat, sun, and wind are part of the performance.** Sweat on skin, hair blown by wind, sun on the face — never cleaned away. A real body in a real place is never spotless.
- **When a stunt double performs the wide action shot, the face in the close-up must be the same actor.** Cut on movement, never on a face that does not match: the wide shot ends mid-motion, the close-up picks up the same motion with the actor's real face. A face that changes between the wide shot and the close-up breaks the scene.

**Check:** every performance in the delivered video is physically possible for a real human, effort is visible where the action is hard, and the face in every close-up matches the body in the wide shot.

## 2. VFX Integration — CG Creatures in Live Footage

A CG creature must live in the same light as the live plate. If the creature looks pasted on, the whole shot fails — no matter how good the creature looks on its own.

Rules:

- **Match the sun.** The creature's skin takes the sun's direction and colour temperature from the live plate: warm light from the same side, cool shadow on the same side. A creature lit from the wrong direction is the first thing the eye catches.
- **The creature casts shadows on real surfaces and reflects in real water.** A creature standing on a beach casts a shadow on the sand; a creature near water appears in the water's reflection. No shadow, no reflection — no presence.
- **The creature gets wet when the scene is wet.** Rain, spray, and splashes touch the creature the same way they touch the actor: wet skin, dripping edges, water running off a shell.
- **The actor's eyeline must hit the creature's eyes.** Nothing is there on set, so the eyeline is planned and held in every shot — a marker, a rehearsed height, a fixed point the actor looks at. The eyeline never drifts between shots: the actor looks at the same height, in the same direction, in every take of the same moment.
- **Scale is fixed.** The creature's size against the actor never drifts between shots. Record the scale once (creature height against actor height, in the shot list) and check it in every shot that shows them together.
- **Contact is real.** A hand pressing a creature's shell must press INTO the surface, never float above it; a foot on a creature must dent what it stands on. If the contact cannot be real on set, the shot is re-framed so the contact is not shown — never faked with a floating hand.

**Check:** every CG creature in the delivered video shares the live plate's light direction and colour temperature, casts shadows and reflections where it should, holds a fixed scale against the actor, and every contact with it is real.

## 3. Water, Wetness & Underwater Continuity — Track the Wet State

Water is a continuity problem before it is a visual one. Track the wet state of every character across cuts: hair wet, damp, or dry; skin glistening or dry; clothes clinging or loose; salt and sand present or washed off.

Rules:

- **The wet state only changes when the story changes it.** A fall in the water makes hair wet; time in the sun dries it. A character who is soaking wet in one shot and dry in the next, with nothing in between, is a continuity defect. Record the wet state per shot in the shot list and check it at every cut.
- **Underwater, the look is fixed.** Blue cast, light rays from above, bubbles, muffled sound — and hair and clothes move as water moves them, never as air does. A dress that falls the way it falls on land is wrong underwater; hair floats, cloth drifts, the body moves slowly.
- **Splashes, spray, and water on the lens are part of the shot, not mistakes to remove.** Water on the camera lens, spray across the frame, a wave hitting the deck — these are the scene. Remove them only when the water on the lens hides the action itself.
- **The ocean may act.** When the water itself is a character — rising, reaching, withdrawing — its behaviour is continuous across shots, like any character's (see the `cinematic-power` skill, system 2).

**Check:** every character's wet state matches the story's last change to it in every shot, underwater shots hold the fixed underwater look with water-driven movement, and no continuity jump in wetness survives to delivery.

## 4. Practical Locations & Set Extension — Never Let the Seam Show

Shoot the real place, extend it with CG, and never let the seam show. The extension shares the location's light, haze, and water line; the horizon, the tide line, and the shadows must continue across the seam without a step.

Rules:

- **One light, one haze, one water line.** The CG extension takes its light direction, colour temperature, haze, and water level from the real location. If the real beach has a hazy horizon, the extended beach has the same haze; if the real tide line is at the actor's knees, the extended water meets the sand at the same height.
- **A built set and its CG extension are one continuous space.** A ship, a village, a deck — the actor walks from the built part into the extended part without the camera ever noticing the join. The join is never placed where the actor crosses it, and the floor, the walls, and the light continue across it.
- **If the extension cannot match the real light, change the extension, never the location.** The real place is the reference; the CG is adjusted to it. Re-lighting the location to suit the extension is backwards.
- **Aerials and wide shots get the same check.** A drone shot over a real landscape with CG extension is checked at the same seam: the horizon, the water, and the shadows must continue without a step, at full frame width.

**Check:** no seam is visible between the real location or set and its CG extension in any delivered shot — light, haze, water line, horizon, and shadows continue across the join.

## 5. Live-Action Stunt & Action Choreography — Planned, Rehearsed, Shot to Look Unplanned

Action is planned, rehearsed, and then shot to look unplanned. Rope swings, falls, fights on a moving boat, fire — each is choreographed beat by beat in the shot list, and the camera hides the safety (rigs, mats, wires) without ever hiding the impact.

Rules:

- **Choreograph beat by beat.** Every stunt has a written sequence in the shot list: the move, the landing, the recovery. Nothing is improvised on the day; improvisation is where people get hurt and where continuity breaks.
- **Impact physics is real.** Dust rises, water sprays, wood breaks, the body recoils. The impact is sold by what the world does around it — and by the sound and the reaction that follow it.
- **The character is always readable in the action.** Fast cuts and motion blur never lose who is who. If the viewer cannot tell which character is falling, the cut is too fast or the framing is too tight — slow it down or widen it.
- **Never fake an impact the body cannot survive.** Sell it with sound, dust, and reaction instead: the camera cuts away, the dust rises, the character reacts — the body is never asked to take a hit it cannot take.

**Check:** every stunt in the delivered video is choreographed in the shot list, the safety is invisible, the impact is physically real, and the character stays readable through the fastest action.

## 6. Real-Animal Performance — The Animal Does What It Does

A real animal on set cannot be directed frame by frame — it does what it does. Plan for many takes, cut around the animal's real behaviour, and let the edit create the comic timing: a look, a pause, a sudden move.

Rules:

- **The animal's weight, gait, and unpredictability are the performance.** A real animal walks, shakes, and looks with a weight no animation reproduces. Never animate over them: the animal's real body is the performance, and the shot is built around what the animal actually did.
- **Cut around the behaviour.** Shoot many takes, then cut to the moments the animal gave you — the look, the pause, the sudden move. Comic timing is made in the edit, not demanded from the animal.
- **CG touch-up is allowed only where the animal cannot perform.** A look that must land on cue, a movement that must match the story — touch up that moment, and only that moment. The touch-up must preserve the animal's real body, never replace it with a cartoon: same size, same gait, same weight.
- **A real animal is not a CG creature.** A real animal follows this system; a CG creature follows the `cinematic-power` skill, system 2. Never mix the two in one animal: the audience can tell.

**Check:** every real animal in the delivered video keeps its real body, weight, and gait in every shot, the comic timing is made in the edit, and any CG touch-up preserves the animal's real body.
