# Video Regeneration

The user uploads a video and asks for it to be regenerated. The reference is the user's own uploaded video, used as the analysis source. The output is a NEW, original video: it preserves the reference's core storytelling concept, emotional impact, visual quality, narrative structure, and overall creative direction — but it is a professionally produced alternative version, not a duplicate. Only minor, explicitly requested or small creative changes are introduced. The reference is never copied frame for frame, and copyrighted footage is never reproduced.

Working order, always: first analyse, then plan, then generate, finally inspect and correct.

Nothing here replaces the existing systems. Characters in the regenerated video are registered characters: identity anchoring, 100% reference fidelity, face-consistency correction, angle-matched verification, and the expression library (`character-consistency` skill) all still apply, unchanged. Outfits follow the outfit-consistency rules (`character-consistency`, Section 13). Camera angles follow the script-driven automatic camera angles system (`production-systems` skill, A12) — the angles for the regenerated video are chosen from its beats, whether the script is supplied or generated. Voices follow the `voice-consistency` skill. This skill adds only the regeneration workflow on top of that foundation.

## 1. Complete Reference Video Analysis — First, Before Anything Else

Before generating anything, analyse the entire uploaded video from beginning to end. Do not skip any scene, transition, action, or important visual detail. If possible, inspect the video frame by frame; otherwise, analyse all available frames and important motion sequences.

Analyse all of the following:

- Overall story, narrative structure, and central message.
- Every scene and its exact chronological order.
- Character identities, facial features, approximate ages, body proportions, hairstyles, clothing, and accessories.
- Character expressions, emotions, gestures, movements, and interactions.
- Objects, props, their locations, and how characters interact with them.
- Camera angles, framing, shot composition, camera movements, zooms, and focus.
- Lighting, shadows, colours, environment, background, and atmosphere.
- Scene transitions, editing rhythm, pacing, and visual continuity.
- Audio, dialogue, background ambience, sound effects, and music mood.
- Opening hook, emotional development, climax, and ending.

Never invent visual details that cannot be verified from the reference. If something cannot be seen or heard in the reference, say so — do not fill the gap with an invention and then build on it.

**Check:** the analysis covers every scene in order, every character, every important object, camera, light, and sound — with nothing invented that the reference does not show.

## 2. Create a New Video, Not a Duplicate

Create a fresh video inspired by the reference. Do not reproduce every frame identically. The new video must feel like a professionally produced alternative version of the reference.

Preserve:

- The original story's central idea.
- The emotional journey and intended audience reaction.
- The overall narrative progression.
- The important visual storytelling elements.
- The realism and professional cinematic quality.

Creatively improve only:

- Minor scene arrangements.
- Background details and environmental elements.
- Small character gestures and natural expressions.
- Camera composition and cinematic movement.
- Minor transitions and storytelling details.
- Visual details that make the new version more engaging.

Do not introduce changes that damage the original story, character motivations, emotional meaning, or logical sequence of events. Any change the user explicitly requests wins over the defaults above — but a requested change never licenses a change to something the user did not ask about.

**Check:** the new video tells the same story with the same emotional journey, is not a frame-for-frame copy, and contains no change that damages the story, motivations, meaning, or sequence.

## 3. Character and Object Continuity

Maintain strict visual continuity throughout the entire video.

Every recurring character must retain the same:

- Facial identity and facial structure.
- Skin appearance and natural facial details.
- Hairstyle and hair colour.
- Body proportions and physical appearance.
- Clothing, accessories, and footwear unless a change is explicitly required by the story.

Every important object must maintain consistent:

- Shape, size, colour, and material.
- Physical location and orientation.
- Ownership and interaction history.

A character must never randomly change identity, age, clothing, or physical appearance between shots. Verify each character and each important object against the reference before delivery (character-consistency, Sections 11 and 15).

**Check:** every character and every important object matches the reference in every shot, unless the story explicitly requires the change.

## 4. Realistic Physical Interactions — Critical

All physical interactions must follow believable real-world physics and anatomy. This is the most common place a regenerated video fails — check it shot by shot.

Hand-to-hand interactions:

- When one character hands money, a gift, a phone, or any object to another character, show a physically correct transfer.
- The receiving hand must visibly approach and make contact with the object.
- The object must remain between the fingers and palm, not pass through the hand.
- The original holder must release the object only after the receiving character has secured it.
- Never allow two characters' hands to merge, overlap unnaturally, or intersect.

Object interactions:

- Objects must never pass through hands, bodies, clothing, tables, walls, or other objects.
- Maintain correct object weight, gravity, balance, and contact.
- Objects must not disappear, duplicate, teleport, or randomly change shape.
- Maintain correct object ownership before, during, and after every interaction.

Body and character interactions:

- No intersecting bodies, fused limbs, distorted fingers, extra fingers, missing limbs, or unnatural anatomy.
- No facial merging or identity blending between characters.
- Maintain realistic walking, sitting, standing, reaching, turning, and movement.
- Ensure that characters occupy physically separate and believable positions.

Every action must have a clear beginning, middle, and completion. A transfer that starts but never visibly finishes is a failed shot.

**Check:** every handover, contact, and movement in every shot is physically possible, complete, and free of merging, intersection, duplication, or disappearance.

## 5. Cinematography and Visual Realism

Use professional live-action filmmaking principles.

Prioritise:

- Photorealistic human characters.
- Natural skin texture and realistic facial expressions.
- Physically accurate lighting and shadows.
- Natural depth of field.
- Consistent perspective and spatial relationships.
- Realistic environmental details.
- Smooth and purposeful camera movements.
- Cinematic but believable composition.

Camera angles follow the script-driven automatic camera angles system (production-systems, A12): each shot's angle is chosen from its beat — story function, emotion, character relationship, what the viewer must notice — never randomly, and never by habit.

Avoid excessive artificial sharpness, plastic-looking skin, exaggerated facial features, unnatural movement, artificial lighting, and synthetic-looking textures. The final result should look like professionally filmed live-action footage, not an obvious AI-generated video.

Do not imitate a specific filmmaker or copy copyrighted footage.

**Check:** the video reads as professionally filmed live-action footage — natural skin, light, and movement, with angles chosen from the beats and no synthetic-looking texture or motion.

## 6. Storyboard and Shot Planning — Before Generation

Before generation, internally organise the new video into a chronological storyboard. Do not generate before the storyboard exists.

For each shot, determine:

- Shot number and approximate duration.
- Characters present.
- Location and background.
- Main action.
- Facial expression and emotional state.
- Camera angle and movement.
- Important objects and interactions.
- Transition to the next shot.

Preserve logical continuity between consecutive shots. Do not create unnecessary random cuts or repetitive shots. The storyboard is part of the regeneration record (`docs/09-video-regeneration.md`, folder layout) so the plan can be checked against the delivered video.

**Check:** a complete chronological storyboard exists before generation, every shot has all eight items above, and consecutive shots connect logically.

## 7. Audio and Sound Design

Analyse the original audio and understand its emotional purpose before creating new audio.

Create suitable audio for the new video:

- Natural environmental ambience.
- Appropriate sound effects.
- Realistic interaction sounds.
- Emotionally appropriate background music.
- Proper synchronisation between visual actions and sound.

Do not reproduce copyrighted songs or exact recordings from the reference. Use original music with a similar emotional atmosphere when appropriate. Avoid unnecessary dialogue, narration, or captions unless they are required by the story.

**Check:** ambience, effects, interaction sounds, and music are present, fit the story's emotion, are synchronised to the picture, and contain no copyrighted recording from the reference.

## 8. Final Quality Control — Before Delivery

Before considering the video complete, review the generated result. This QC pass is mandatory — it is not optional, and it is not skipped when time is short.

Check for:

- Character identity consistency.
- Facial and body distortions.
- Hand and finger abnormalities.
- Object intersections and incorrect transfers.
- Floating, disappearing, or duplicated objects.
- Clothing and accessory changes.
- Background inconsistencies.
- Lighting and shadow inconsistencies.
- Unnatural camera movement.
- Audio synchronisation errors.
- Broken scene transitions.
- Storytelling and chronological errors.

If any defect is detected, correct or regenerate the affected shot whenever the available tools support it. Never intentionally accept a visibly defective shot as a finished professional result. Do not deliver a video that fails a check without telling the user what failed (production-systems, A11).

**Check:** every item above was checked on the delivered video, and every defect found was corrected, regenerated, or reported to the user — no defective shot was silently accepted.

## 9. Final Output Requirements

Match the reference video's aspect ratio, general pacing, and intended platform unless the user specifies otherwise.

Preserve the original video's approximate duration when technically possible. If the generation model has a shorter duration limit, divide the story into coherent, sequential clips. Maintain consistent visual quality across all clips.

Do not sacrifice realism, continuity, or physical accuracy merely to produce the video faster.

Working order, restated: first analyse, then plan, then generate, finally inspect and correct.

**Check:** aspect ratio, pacing, platform, and approximate duration match the reference (or the user's stated change); clips are coherent and sequential where the story was divided; quality is consistent across every clip.
